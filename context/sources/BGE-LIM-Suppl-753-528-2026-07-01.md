Dear Mr. Johnston:

> Baltimore Gas and Electric Company ("BGE" or the "Company") files herewith Supplement 753 to Md. E-6 and Supplement 528 to Md. G-9 to introduce BGE's Limited Income Discount Mechanism. With this filing, BGE has accelerated its system work to deliver the Limited Income Discount on an accelerated basis to address affordability concerns for certain residential customers in the Company's service territory who qualify based on the Maryland Office of Home Energy Program's ("OHEP") requirements, subject to approval by the Maryland Public Service Commission ("Commission"). Approval of BGE's request, which facilitates acceleration of the discount to eligible limited income customers, still allows the Commission and the PC 59 working group additional time in 2026 to determine what portion of the total discount should be recovered from residential customers and what remaining portion of the total discount should be recovered from commercial and industrial ("C&I") customers. Accelerating implementation allows BGE to provide near-term bill relief to eligible customers during periods of elevated usage and costs, including the current summer cooling season. BGE is focused on affordability, especially given current electricity supply prices driving higher customer bills, the significantly colder weather in recent winters, and the current summer cooling season.

## Background

> On February 12, 2026, the Commission approved the general design of the Limited Income Mechanism, in Order No. 92190, including a plan to include proposed marketing materials in Summer 2026 and proposed tariffs in Fall 2026. The Commission's Order directed the PC59 Work Group to address technical implementation details, customer education, and marketing plans to meet the directed timeline.
>
> The Work Group has been able to efficiently address implementation details resulting in the Company being able to accelerate implementation of the discount, as explained in this filing. While the Company plans to implement the associated recovery surcharge later this year, the Company is making this filing now to begin providing the discount to accelerate these critical benefits to those customers qualifying through OHEP. The implementation of the discount has been a collaborative project requiring communication between BGE, OHEP and many other stakeholders. BGE appreciates the work and cooperation between all Work Group parties and appreciates the Commission for consideration of the Company's instant proposal.

## Proposed Mechanism

> - **Eligibility**: Customers identified by OHEP in Poverty Levels 1 through 5 will be eligible for the Limited Income Discount. The Company has confirmed that OHEP will be able to provide the Company a file for initial enrollment and continued weekly enrollment files starting August 1st. Customers eligible via OHEP will not need to take any additional steps to be enrolled in BGE's Limited Income Discount.
> - **Applicable Period**: The Limited Income Discount will apply for 18 months from the time that OHEP indicates a customer is eligible.[^1]
> - **Exclusions**: The Limited Income Discount will not apply to master-metered customers. Phase II of the Work Group plans to review master metered accounts.
> - **Appearance on Bill**: The calculated discount will appear on the eligible customer's bill as the "Limited Income Discount" and will reduce the total amount due.[^2]

[^1]: Commission Order No. 92420. May 26, 2026.

[^2]: Customers who are not eligible for the discount will not have a discount line item on their bill.

## Discount Calculation

> The Work Group relied on Fiscal Year 2025 data for its report to the Commission in October of 2025.[^3] At this time, Fiscal Year 2026 data is not readily available. Therefore, BGE proposes to base the initial discount rates on the same data used for the Work Group report filed on October 1, 2025. BGE will update the discount rates for Fiscal Year 2026 information following any agreed-upon timelines with the Work Group to align with other utilities throughout the state of Maryland.
>
> Discount rates as calculated in Attachment 1 rely on the average income, average benefits, and average usage for each group of customers from Fiscal Year 2025, as provided by OHEP. Additionally, the discount calculation uses the most recent 12 months of distribution and supply rates to calculate an average energy bill.
>
> As approved by the Commission, the discount reflects a discount of the distribution and supply rate – regardless of whether the customer takes supply from BGE or a third party supplier.
>
> The calculated discount rates are differentiated by OHEP Poverty Level and OHEP-determined heating source.
>
> For each category, an energy burden of 3% or 6% is used depending on heating source.

[^3]: PC59 Limited Income Mechanism for Utility Customers – Report. Prepared for the Commission by PC59 Work Group. October 1, 2025.

| Service Type | Heating Source | Energy Burden |
| ------------ | -------------- | ------------- |
| Electric     | Electric       | 6%            |
| Electric     | Non-Electric   | 3%            |
| Gas          | Gas            | 3%            |
| Gas          | Non-Gas        | 3%            |

> To calculate the discount rate for each category of customer, 3% or 6% of the average annual income for customers within that category is compared to the average annual distribution and supply bill after benefits are applied. This amount is then divided by the estimated throughput for that category to determine a rate per kwh or therm. This calculation results in a rate per kWh or therm that targets a 6% total energy burden for the customer type on average.
>
> The Company's proposed tariff is included as Attachment 2 and includes the calculated discount rates based on the Fiscal Year 2025 data used for the Work Group's 2025 Report.

## Marketing and Communications

> The Company will implement a comprehensive, multi-channel communication and education strategy to support the rollout and ongoing administration of the Limited Income Discount Program. BGE's plan, included as Attachment 3, uses existing communication channels at no additional cost and includes bill inserts, website updates, social media, news releases, fact sheet community engagement, and customer service outreach. The plan was presented to the PC59 Work Group and received positive feedback.
>
> Customer education is critical because eligibility for the Limited Income Discount depends on active OHEP enrollment. Since the discount expires after 18 months unless OHEP eligibility is recertified, BGE will provide clear, accessible educational materials that explain program eligibility, the relationship between the discount and existing assistance programs, and the expiration and recertification process. Materials will be available in multiple formats to meet diverse customer needs, and customer service representatives will receive targeted training to address inquiries. Through these communication and education efforts, BGE aims to increase awareness, support continued OHEP participation, and help eligible customers maintain access to the Limited Income Discount.

## Deferred Recovery

> As stated previously, the Company's instant proposal includes only the discount being provided to eligible limited income customers. The Limited Income Program surcharge will be filed at the time the Company's IT enhancements are implemented and the Work Group has discussed surcharge cost allocation approaches further. Notably, the Commission's Order No. 92190 directed the Work Group to ensure that C&I customers are included in communications regarding the allocation of costs to recover for the Limited Income Discount Program.
>
> As the Company expects the Commission to approve implementation of the Limited Income Program surcharge within a few short months later this year, the Company is not seeking carrying costs for the short delay in the implementation of the recovery surcharge. Discounts provided prior to the implementation of the recovery surcharge will be included in a future surcharge calculation and recovered from customers through that surcharge mechanism. Of note, the HB 1532 Utility Relief Act allocates $38 million to the initial year of the Limited Income Program. The Company will also include information for customers through marketing and communications to address questions regarding the surcharge.

## Conclusion

> The Company appreciates the Commission's consideration of this filing and the PC59 Work Group's efficiency, which has helped BGE to accelerate this aspect of the Maryland Limited Income Program. Thus, BGE hereby asks the Commission to approve its request to accelerate the provision of the proposed discount rates to eligible limited income customers, with the understanding that the Commission will decide later in 2026 what portion of the total discount should be recovered from residential customers and what remaining portion of the total discount should be recovered from C&I customers, with such costs to be recovered through a customer surcharge mechanism. Note that the proposed tariff is being presented without effective dates so that the Commission is able to consider this tariff at a time that it deems appropriate. BGE is able to implement the tariff immediately, pending Commission approval.
>

> The applicable LID rate shall apply as a reduction to eligible customers' bills. The LID will apply for 18 months beginning with OHEP's determination of eligibility for a customer. The Company shall begin applying the LID with the first billing cycle following the application of eligibility in the Company's billing system. In the next billing cycle following a customer's recertification with OHEP, the 18-month LID eligibility period for that customer will be reset. At the conclusion of 18 months, if a customer has not recertified eligibility with OHEP, the LID will no longer be applied.
>
> ### Calculation of LID Rates
>
> Following the initial implementation of the LID Program, LID rates for the 12-month period beginning January 1 of each year will be determined and filed with the Commission for each residential electric schedule by OHEP benefit level and heating source category.
>
> **Annual Energy Burden**: For each OHEP benefit level and heating source category, OHEP will provide BGE with an average annual income based on the prior fiscal year, which runs from July 1 – June 30. For electric customers with an electric heating source, as determined by OHEP, the energy burden will be calculated as six percent (6.0%) of the average annual income for that Category. For electric customers with a non-electric heating source, as determined by OHEP, the energy burden will be calculated as three percent (3.0%) of the average annual income for that Category.
>
> **Average Annual Electric Billings**: For each Category, the OHEP average annual usage will be used to calculate the average electric bills. The average annual electric billings will be calculated using the past 12-month period average electric supply rate and the distribution rates for Schedule R effective at the time of the filing applied to the average annual usage by Category.
>
> For each Category, the LID rate will be calculated as the difference between the calculated annual energy burden and the average annual electric billings, after considering current average OHEP benefits for that Category, divided by the average annual usage for customers in that Category.
